# V15 style and source notes

- This release builds on v14, retaining all 25 existing scientific slides and navigation mechanics. The cover has two progressive reveals: coauthors, then roadmap.
- Shared heading and body sizes are normalized with `src/styles/v15-polish.css`. Secondary labels remain smaller than principal exposition; math SVGs are visually reviewed rather than indiscriminately enlarged.
- Diagram nodes and network edges are emphasized according to semantic importance (background versus planted/selected). Dense graphs remain more restrained than sparse graphs.
- The 40 positions in slide 7 are a fixed, **illustrative**, non-representative population; they are not observed individual political preferences. The political point cloud is a continuous, center-weighted 2D illustration rather than four synthetic groups.
- The one-dimensional left-right concentration is informed qualitatively by national political-attitude data; there is NO claim that the unmeasured joint distribution with authoritarian/libertarian orientations matches the picture. References: Allianz Research, *Allianz Pulse 2025*, Fig. 10, https://www.allianz.com/content/dam/onemarketing/azcom/Allianz_com/economic-research/publications/specials/en/2025/june/2025-06-25-Allianz-Pulse.pdf ; ESS/Atlas of Social Attitudes, Left-right political position, https://www.atlasofsocialattitudes.eu/indicators/lrscale-left-right-political-position .
- Torino background retains the preexisting Wikimedia Commons OpenStreetMap reference and attribution; because the asset is remote, an offline HTML presentation may display the neutral background instead of the map. We did not invent a substitute street map.
- More interest-moderate/low individuals than high interest is a visual pedagogical hypothesis, not a population statistic. Ages are not linked causally with interest.
- The bibliography on slide 6 now reuses the slide 5 layout pattern.
